Question 1: If a ball in play strikes a protective helmet placed on the ground behind the wicket-keeper, what is the umpire ruling?

menu_book Official Legal Rationale & Concordance

Under MCC Law 28.3.2, if the ball in play strikes a protective helmet placed behind the wicket-keeper, the ball becomes immediately dead and 5 penalty runs are awarded to the batting side in addition to any runs completed. The umpire shall signal dead ball and award the penalty runs immediately.

Under MCC Law 41.16.1 (2022 Code), the bowler may attempt to run out the non-striker from the moment the ball comes into play until the instant when the bowler would normally have been expected to release the ball. Previous editions referencing front-foot impact are superseded.

MCC-2022-REV
§ LAW 28.3.2 BALL IN PLAY / HELMET

“If the ball while in play strikes a protective helmet placed on the ground, the ball shall immediately become dead and the umpire shall award 5 penalty runs to the batting side... runs completed by the batsmen before the ball struck the helmet shall be scored, together with the run in progress if the batsmen had crossed.”

Jurisdiction: All T20I / ODI / Tests MCC Law 28.3
§ LAW 41.16.1 NON-STRIKER LEAVING GROUND

“If the non-striker is out of his/her ground from the moment the ball comes into play to the instant when the bowler would normally have been expected to release the ball, the bowler is permitted to run him/her out. In these circumstances, the non-striker will be Out Run out.”

Updated: October 2022 Code Law 41.16
